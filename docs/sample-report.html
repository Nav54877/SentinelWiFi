CRITICAL Possible fake WiFi network (evil twin) (−20)
SSID 'dlink-Home-2.4' is being broadcast by 2 different access points (C4:AD:34:12:9B:F0, F0:9F:C2:77:AA:01). If this is YOUR network, one of them may be an evil twin — verify which BSSID is your real router (check the sticker on it).
Fix: Before entering any password, check your device's saved network details and compare the router's MAC (BSSID) with the sticker on the physical router. When in doubt, use mobile data.

WARNING Router admin page uses HTTP (not HTTPS) (−15)
When you log into your router at http://192.168.1.1, your admin password travels in plain text — anyone on your WiFi who is listening can read it.
Fix: If the router offers HTTPS admin access, enable it; otherwise avoid managing the router from untrusted devices on the network.

WARNING Network name reveals your router model (−10)
'dlink-Home-2.4' hints at the router brand/model. Attackers use that to look up known default passwords and known weaknesses for that exact device.
Fix: Rename your network to something neutral in the router settings (no brand, no family name, no address).

WARNING 3 risky service(s) exposed on your devices (−10)
Some of your devices offer services like Telnet, FTP or remote desktop to anyone connected to your WiFi. On an open or compromised network these are easy entry points: 192.168.1.1 exposes TR-069 router mgmt on port 7547; 192.168.1.1 exposes Telnet (cleartext login) on port 23; 192.168.1.42 exposes SMB file sharing on port 445.
Fix: Disable unused services in each device's settings (router: turn off Telnet/TR-069/remote admin; PCs: disable SMBv1, close RDP unless you use it). Re-scan to confirm.
